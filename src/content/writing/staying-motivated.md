Motivation is a great way to start something and a terrible way to finish it. It shows up
for the exciting first weekend and vanishes somewhere around the unglamorous middle. For
anything longer than a few days, I've learned to rely on machinery instead.

## Small, visible progress

I keep a running changelog, even for projects nobody uses yet. Seeing the list grow is a
more reliable fuel than any burst of inspiration. Progress you can see is progress you
keep making.

## A stopping point, not a finish line

I try to end each session at a spot that's easy to pick back up — a failing test I know
how to fix, a half-written function. Starting cold is where projects go to die.

## Let it be boring

The middle of a project is supposed to be boring. That's not a sign something's wrong;
it's the part where the work actually happens. The machinery is what carries you through
when the novelty is gone.
