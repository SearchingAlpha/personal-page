Last year I gave myself a rule: ship something, however small, every single month. Not
finish — *ship*. Put it somewhere a stranger could use it.

The first few months were humbling. My idea of a "small" project turned out to be a
three-month project wearing a trench coat. The deadline didn't make me faster; it made
me honest about scope.

## Wanting less, on purpose

The real skill I built wasn't speed. It was learning to want less from version one. A
project that does one thing and exists beats a project that does everything and doesn't.

## The compounding part

Twelve small things shipped is a portfolio. It's also twelve chances to learn a tool, a
pattern, a mistake. The monthly cadence turned out to be a learning engine disguised as
a shipping habit.
